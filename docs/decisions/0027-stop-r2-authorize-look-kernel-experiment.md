# Stop R2; authorize one quarantined look-kernel experiment

## Decision authority

Peter Permenter reviewed the R2 result and the recommendation to stop the epoch,
preserve its useful carrier evidence, leave its final-proof branch unmerged,
and return Nomos to the visual-authoring question that motivated the project.
On 2026-08-31 he replied: `I agree with your recommendation. Let's do that.`

This record writes that disposition into Nomos authority. It selects the exact
`stop R2` verdict already available under `R2.md` section 11 and authorizes one
separately falsifiable quarantined look-kernel experiment. It does not accept
R2, repair its contract, merge its final-evidence candidate, admit a later
runtime epoch, or authorize game adoption.

## Evidence considered

R1 remains the accepted and closed repository runtime baseline under decision
0019 and `RUNTIME.md` revision 4. Nothing in this decision reopens or amends it.

R2 began from a real adopter-neutral finding. Its strict carrier and compiler
preserve already-resolved terrain layers, independent actor facts, and supplied
action availability without becoming a second gameplay authority. Its isolated
viewer consumes only the compiled plan and a renderer-owned catalog, and an
independent author produced a semantically different second scene without
editing the compiler or viewer machinery. Those are useful observed results.

The result remained visually inadequate. The exact committed contact sheet is
70,660 bytes at `2560x720` with SHA-256
`b76edbd9dd03fce5a99c074200ee7311bf87d5d2e5829c800170c129d00bf576`.
The owner's exact recorded visual verdict on PR #201 was `Agreed. Rejected`.
The viewer maps finite facts to isolated boxes, cylinders, rings, and markers.
The proof therefore established deterministic fact carriage and a deterministic
scene graph, but did not establish an owner-accepted visual family. The record
does not infer a more specific aesthetic judgment from that brief verdict.

Draft PR #201 preserves the exact unmerged final-evidence candidate
`ad99db0daa3215a7f3af56540c6081bb12ce3121`, tree
`60c49a38ed22d6db331c23831b392ce06b05a735`. Its applicable reference-host
author and non-author proofs and hosted lanes completed, while detached hosted
job `99070385152` remains supplemental red evidence under the owner's recorded
applicability ruling. The visual rejection independently makes the exact
candidate ineligible for `accept R2`.

That PR also preserves owner-authorized R2 contract-repair records 0024 through
0026 and R2 revision 4. They repair only the unmerged final-proof boundary and
do not change the frozen visual family. This decision does not import those
files into `main`, relabel their evidence, or erase their history. Number 0027
is deliberate: the three earlier numbers remain bound to their exact records
on PR #201 even though that candidate does not merge.

## Finding

Deterministic artifacts and coherent art are separate observed properties. R1
proved that multiple areas can share one bounded but crude visual vocabulary.
R2 proved that a stricter generic observation carrier can preserve supplied
facts and render two mechanically related scenes. Neither proved that an LLM
can author production-representative scenes which remain desirable members of
one executable visual language.

The missing evidence is below the adopter boundary and above raw rendering. A
future experiment must make visual law executable: final-form representative
assets, materials, camera, lighting, effects, whole-scene adjacency,
transitions, hierarchy, density, focal priority, and gameplay-scale readability
must be owned by one frozen kit and compiler rather than improvised per scene or
left as prose around generated target images.

More R2 proof work cannot answer that question. Repairing the rejected catalog
inside R2 would broaden an epoch whose bounded purpose was fact carriage, turn a
generic adopter observation into an art-direction program, and discard the
clean stop condition its contract already provides.

## R2 disposition

**Stop R2.**

R2 is closed as an unadmitted experiment. No R2 repair, rerun, final-evidence
implementation, candidate refresh, or later R2 target is authorized.

The landed R2-1 and R2-2 bytes on `main` remain isolated historical evidence.
They are not accepted R1 surface, an admitted R2 result, a production renderer,
or an adopter dependency. They may be read as prior evidence; they may not be
extended under decision 0023.

Draft PR #201 remains unmerged historical evidence at its exact head and tree.
Its proof outputs, owner-authorized contract-repair records, retained red runs,
passing runs, and rejected visual digest keep their recorded meanings. Closing
the PR or issue after this decision lands is repository housekeeping, not
evidence deletion; the branch and Git objects remain historical references.

## Authorized look-kernel experiment

Authorize one quarantined experiment under `experiments/`, governed by issue
#205 and beginning only after this decision lands.

This is the narrow executable exception that decision 0014 did not authorize.
Decision 0014's static target pack and its recorded verdict remain unchanged;
the exception permits only this quarantined look-kernel experiment. It is not
a Gate 0 pass, Gate 1 work, renderer architecture, production-art approval, a
new Gate K attempt, or authority for any other executable visual experiment.

Its question is exact:

> Can cold LLM authors create two materially different scenes in addition to
> one reference scene, using one immutable executable visual kit, with
> content-only edits and deterministic compiled artifacts, such that the owner
> accepts all three actual-play-size frames as coherent, readable, desirable
> members of one visual family?

The experiment starts from the existing Gate 0 gaol pack as taste evidence. The
generated bitmaps are references, not meshes, textures, materials, or accepted
runtime inputs. The experiment must produce and freeze the executable visual
kit it actually uses.

### Required boundary

The frozen kit owns the tested slice's representative geometry or other final
renderable assets, material and palette families, camera, lighting, actor
assemblies, effects, UI marks where present, and deterministic variation rules.
The compiler owns whole-scene presentation consequences including adjacency,
junctions, transitions, composition hierarchy, landmark visibility, density,
and gameplay-scale readability rules. The renderer executes the compiled scene
and cannot repair content or improvise undeclared style.

The content author uses bounded semantic and presentation intent. Raw
transforms, shaders, arbitrary payloads, per-scene material definitions, and
renderer code are outside that lane. A new scene cannot add or edit an asset,
kit rule, compiler, renderer, shader, UI module, or test expectation.

The experiment uses its own quarantined fixture and artifact grammar. It does
not import R1 or R2 code, plans, schemas, fixtures, or generated artifacts as a
dependency; those lines are comparison evidence only. It adds no Cargo
workspace member, accepted crate or app, schema register, gameplay fact,
action-legality rule, collision or navigation rule, persistence surface, or
other gameplay authority.

Candidate generation may use stochastic tools only in a separately recorded,
quarantined kit-author lane. A candidate enters the frozen experiment kit only
after review and content-addressing. From that point, content compilation and
runtime rendering make no model or remote-generation call and consume only the
frozen bytes. The experiment records candidate provenance without claiming
that a seed makes a generative model reproducible.

### Falsifiable acceptance

The later experiment issue must freeze exact inputs, outputs, commands,
authoring packets, budgets, and a visual rubric before implementation. At
minimum it requires:

1. one reference scene rendered with the intended representative technique;
   placeholder primitive blockouts cannot satisfy visual acceptance;
2. two independently authored scenes that differ materially in topology,
   composition, and landmark arrangement while using the same frozen kit;
3. two cold authors who each receive only the declared packet and change
   content files only, with zero substantive operator hint and no retry
   disguised as a first attempt;
4. byte-identical compiled scene and asset-selection artifacts for the same
   exact inputs, kit version, and compiler;
5. no runtime network or model call, unrecorded source, raw-transform escape,
   or scene-specific machinery edit;
6. actual-play-size frames and one contact sheet bound to the exact source,
   kit, compiler, renderer, viewport, and environment;
7. measured time to first valid compile, validation cycles, time to first
   visible frame, time to visual submission, files changed, output size, and
   peak build disk, recorded as observations unless the issue justifies a
   specific ceiling; and
8. an owner verdict of `accepted` or `rejected` on each frame and the family as
   a whole, followed by a non-author reproduction and boundary audit.

All three frames must be accepted. Mechanical validity, artifact identity, or
an average score cannot offset one rejected frame.

### Experiment stop line

Stop for owner disposition on any:

- rejected reference or cold-authored frame;
- required asset, kit, compiler, renderer, shader, UI, or test-source edit for
  either new scene;
- content-authored raw transform, shader, arbitrary payload, or unbounded style
  vocabulary;
- stochastic or remote generation in the frozen content-build or runtime path;
- adopter identity, payload, palette, coordinate selection, mechanic, or
  governance entering the experiment;
- content-specific patch presented as a reusable rule;
- non-reproducible compiled artifact, undeclared input, red required proof, or
  missing observation; or
- attempt to promote experiment bytes directly into accepted R1 or a later
  epoch.

Failure is evidence about the kit or thesis. It is not permission to widen the
input language, add room-specific escape hatches, or move the visual judgment
behind more proof infrastructure.
